Visa Bulletin movement

See how each cutoff date in the Visa Bulletin moved over the last month, 3 months or 6 months. Green means the date moved forward, red means it moved backward, and blue means no change.

General information only, not legal advice. A cutoff date moving forward doesn’t mean your case is approved. Confirm every date in the official Visa Bulletin. Not sure which chart applies to you? USCIS says each month whether to use Final Action Dates or Dates for Filing for adjustment of status.

How to read this

Each card is one category. Every row shows the new cutoff date and how far it moved since the month you pick: last month, 3 months ago or 6 months ago. You are eligible only if your priority date is earlier than the cutoff date. “Current” means anyone in that category can proceed. Read more in what the Visa Bulletin is and what Visa Bulletin movement means. To test your own date, use the priority date checker.

Good to know

  • Dates can move forward, stay still, or move backward. A backward move is called retrogression. A category that was current and now has a date also counts as backward.
  • Unavailable means no visa numbers can be issued in that category for that country that month.
  • Movement over 3 or 6 months compares only the two bulletins, so a date that moved up and then back down in between shows as the net change.
  • Rest of World means every country not listed separately. China means mainland China born.
  • For F2A, the bulletin has extra notes about applicants who are exempt from the per-country limit. Read the notes in the official bulletin.
