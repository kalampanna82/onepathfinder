CSPA age calculator

Estimate a child’s age under the Child Status Protection Act (CSPA), which can keep a child from “aging out” of a green card case.

Estimate only. This is general information, not legal advice, and it can’t guarantee any outcome. Results are only as accurate as the dates you enter.

Who this is for

  • Works for: children of permanent residents (the F2A category) and derivative children listed on a family-sponsored or employment-based petition. For a derivative child, use the parent’s petition dates.
  • Not for: immediate relatives of U.S. citizens, diversity visa cases, and refugee or asylee derivatives. They follow different rules.

What is it? The day USCIS received the petition. For most family cases that is Form I-130. For employment cases it is Form I-140.

Which paper? The Form I-797C, Notice of Action, also called the receipt notice. USCIS mails it after it accepts the petition. Use the Received Date printed on it. This is not the same as the date of the notice.

For a child listed on a parent’s petition, use the parent’s petition.

What is it? The day USCIS approved the petition.

Which paper? The Form I-797, Notice of Action that says Approval Notice near the top. The date of that notice is usually the approval date. Your online USCIS case status also shows when the petition was approved.

Don’t use the day the notice arrived in the mail. If your papers show different dates, or you’re unsure, ask a licensed immigration attorney or DOJ-accredited representative.

Use the Final Action Dates chart. Tap the question mark to see how to find it.

What is the Visa Bulletin? A monthly publication from the U.S. Department of State. For each visa category and country, it lists a cutoff date. If your priority date is earlier than the cutoff, a visa is available. It has two charts: Final Action Dates and Dates for Filing. For CSPA age, use Final Action Dates.

How to check when your visa became available:

  1. Find your priority date on the Form I-797 approval notice.
  2. Open the Visa Bulletin and go to the Final Action Dates chart for family-sponsored (or employment-based) cases.
  3. Find your category (for a child of a permanent resident, F2A) and your country column. Most people use “All Chargeability Areas.” Some countries, such as India, Mexico and the Philippines, have their own columns. For a derivative child, use the parent’s category and country.
  4. If your priority date is earlier than the date shown, a visa was available that month. “C” means current, and “U” means unavailable.
  5. Go back through earlier bulletins, which are in the archive on the same site, to find the first month when that was true. Pick that bulletin’s month and year here.

Which day is counted? The first day of that bulletin’s month. Use the month named on the bulletin, for example “December 2024,” even though it is published earlier. If the petition was approved after that day, the approval date is used instead. The calculator does this for you.

How the estimate works

  1. The visa availability date is the later of two dates: the petition approval date, or the first day of the Visa Bulletin month when the priority date became current.
  2. We work out the child’s age on that date.
  3. We subtract the number of days the petition was pending, counted from the filing date to the approval date.
  4. If the result is under 21, the child is under 21 under the CSPA formula. The child must also take a qualifying step to seek permanent residence within one year of the visa availability date.

For a full walkthrough with an example, read How to calculate CSPA age yourself.

Rules that can change the answer

This tool uses the Final Action Dates chart. USCIS requires that chart for adjustment of status applications filed on or after August 15, 2025. Applications that were already pending before that date may be treated under an earlier, more favorable policy. If that might apply to you, speak with a licensed immigration attorney or DOJ-accredited representative. Policies can change, so check the sources below.
