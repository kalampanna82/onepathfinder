EAD renewal planner

Most work permit (EAD) renewals no longer get an automatic extension. If your new card is not approved by the time the old one expires, you may have to stop working. Enter your card’s expiry date and the current USCIS processing time to see when to file and whether a gap is likely.

What changed. For EAD renewals filed on or after October 30, 2025, USCIS no longer extends your work permit automatically while the renewal is pending. DHS made this final in a rule effective October 14, 2026 (Federal Register 2026-20955). A receipt notice alone does not let you keep working after your card expires. Exceptions include TPS, E and L spouses and F-1 STEM OPT. The rule is being challenged in court, so check for news before you rely on it.

A calendar, not a decision. USCIS decides when your card is approved, and processing times change every month. These dates are estimates. Talk to your employer and an immigration attorney early. This is general information, not legal advice.

The category is printed on your card, for example “C09” or “A12”.

The “Card expires” date on the front of your card.

Look it up on USCIS processing times: choose Form I-765, your category and office, and enter the number of months shown. It changes every month.

The “Received date” on your Form I-797C receipt notice.

The rules behind these dates

  • When to file. USCIS says you can file Form I-765 to renew your EAD up to 180 days before it expires. Filing early does not cut short your current card.
  • No automatic extension for most renewals. If you filed on or after October 30, 2025, your work permit ends on the date on your card unless the new card is approved first. Renewals filed before October 30, 2025 keep any automatic extension they already had.
  • TPS. TPS EADs can still be extended automatically by law or by a Federal Register notice for your country. Check the USCIS page for your country.
  • E and L spouses are work authorized because of their status, and STEM OPT students who file on time keep an extension of up to 180 days.
  • A gap means no work. Your employer has to re-check your work permit (Form I-9) when your card expires. Plan ahead with them.
