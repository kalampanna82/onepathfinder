USCIS filing fees by form

What the government charges to file the most common immigration forms, grouped by category. Search by form number or name.

Confirm before you pay. Fees change, and USCIS rejects a filing with the wrong fee. These amounts were last checked on October 22, 2026, and include the changes for requests postmarked on or after October 16, 2026. Always confirm on the official USCIS fee schedule (G-1055). This is general information, not legal advice. It lists government fees only, not attorney or service fees.

A dash (—) in the Online column means no online fee is listed here, or the form is not filed online. Check USCIS for that form. Many forms cost $50 less when filed online with a USCIS account.

Other costs to expect

  • Employer add-on fees. Most I-129 and I-140 petitions also carry an Asylum Program Fee ($600, $300 for small employers, none for nonprofits). Some H-1B and L-1 petitions have further fees. These are in addition to the amounts above.
  • Biometrics. Biometric services are included in the fees above for most forms, but a few categories, such as TPS, can still add a biometric fee.
  • Fee waivers. Some applicants can ask USCIS to waive a fee with Form I-912. Not every form qualifies, and some fees cannot be waived.
  • Other agencies. The SEVIS fee, visa application fees and medical exam costs are paid separately. See the SEVIS fee calculator.
  • Mailing date counts. For mailed filings, the postmark date decides which fee applies, not the date USCIS receives the package.
